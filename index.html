A reading map for Gospel Doctrine

Isaiah, night to dawn

Sixty-six chapters fall into five movements — from a rebellious nation warned to repent, through the Assyrian siege of Jerusalem, to a scattered Israel gathered home and a light finally offered to every nation.

1Overview 2–35Repent or die 36–39The bridge 40–55Gathering 56–66Gentiles
01 Before ~700 BC — the introduction
Isaiah 1

The big picture

  • The people of Israel are apostate, rebellious, and corrupt; only a few remain faithful.
  • Their sacrifices and feasts are rejected.
  • They are called to repent and work righteousness.
  • Zion will be redeemed in the day of restoration.
02 ~700 BC — before the Assyrian conquest
Isaiah 2–35

Repent or die

  • Israel and Judah still have an opportunity to repent, but Isaiah warns of coming destruction and Assyrian conquest.
03 ~700 BC — the Assyrian invasion
Isaiah 36–39

The bridge (from 2 Kings 18–20)

  • The Assyrians attack. Jerusalem is threatened.
  • These chapters connect the first and second halves of Isaiah.
04 After the kingdoms are destroyed — post–700 BC
Isaiah 40–55

Gathering & redemption

  • The focus shifts from warning Israel about destruction to God's promise to redeem and gather scattered Israel.
05 Later prophetic horizon — post–700 BC
Isaiah 56–66

Israel + Gentiles

  • The gathering expands. Gentiles are invited into the covenant, the temple, and the house of Israel.
  • Israel's scattering ultimately becomes a blessing to the nations.

Structure: chs. 1 (overview) · 2–35 (warning) · 36–39 (historical bridge, parallel to 2 Kings 18–20) · 40–55 (redemption) · 56–66 (Israel and the Gentiles).
